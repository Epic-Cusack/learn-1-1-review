Learn 1.1 · Reading to Understand

Engage, Learn, Practice

In this lesson, you will learn why people read and what good readers do. Work from the top of the page to the bottom.

ContentI can summarize the main idea.LanguageI can use the Mark and Explain strategy to actively read.

How to use the activities: Select Start. Use Listen or Read for support. Complete the activity. Use the arrows. Return here and scroll down.

1

What Do I Read?

Think about what you read every day. Watch the video and explore.

Think: What do you read?

Review focus: engagement, clear visual focus, media labels synchronized with narration, functional answer feedback.
2

Words You Need

Learn important words before you continue. Select each word for its meaning, picture, and example.

Think: Which words are new to you?

Review focus: first-day EL access, concise definitions, picture–word association, consistent pronunciation practice.
3

Why Do We Read?

Explore four reasons people read. Reveal each missing reason and complete the practice questions.

Think: Why do you read?

Review focus: scratch interaction has an equivalent Reveal control; reasons remain distinct in examples and feedback.
4

What Do Readers Do?

Learn three actions: look before reading, make a guess, and mark and explain.

Think: Which reading action will you try?

Review focus: sequence is explicit; every interaction produces usable feedback; age-appropriate visuals and language.
5

Mark and Explain

Read about Farid. Mark an important detail, explain it, and write the idea in your own words.

Think: How can marking a text help you understand?

Review focus: progression increases student output without adding an unrelated skill; drag tasks include keyboard-accessible alternatives.
6

Practice

Read Trinh Takes the Test. Practice reading, marking, speaking, and writing about a new story.

Think: Which strategy helped you most?

Review focus: scaffold release, oral rehearsal, written production, and student-selected supports.

Knowledge Check

Thabo Fixes the Controller

Thabo is a ninth-grade student from South Africa.
He enjoys playing video games with his friends.
One day, a button on his controller stops working.
Thabo reads an online repair guide.
He follows the steps and cleans under the button.
The controller works again!
Thabo can play with his friends.
1. Would you try to fix something that stopped working? Why or why not?

I would / would not try to fix something because ______.

2. Thabo reads a guide to help him fix his controller.
3. What is Thabo’s main reason for reading the repair guide?
4. What is the best summary of Thabo Fixes the Controller?
